## 2. Brand Foundation

### 2.1 Brand name

**Primary spelling:** BABAJAN SUMANGALI\
**Display treatment:** "BABAJAN" as the dominant word; "SUMANGALI"
beneath it with generous letter spacing.\
**Do not:** Change the spelling, merge the words, abbreviate the name in
customer-facing copy, or add an unapproved slogan to the logo.

Use the full name in formal brand contexts. "Babajan Sumangali" may be
used in natural sentence case in body copy and metadata.

### 2.2 Brand proposition

Babajan Sumangali brings together considered fashion for women and men:
pieces that respect cultural roots while feeling current, confident, and
personal.

### 2.3 Brand promise

**Clothing that helps every person express their story with
confidence.**

This is an internal strategic promise, not a mandatory tagline. Use the
approved tagline below for consistent brand-facing use.

### 2.4 Primary tagline

**Wear Your Story**

### 2.5 Optional campaign lines

Use selectively in campaigns, not as permanent logo text: - Tradition,
Styled for Today - Made for Your Moments - Elegance in Every Detail -
Find the Piece That Feels Like You - Clothing That Connects Generations

Do not use every line at once. One campaign should have one clear
message.

### 2.6 Brand essence

**Modern tradition. Personal expression. Considered elegance.**

### 2.7 Brand values

1.  **Elegance:** Refined rather than flashy.
2.  **Cultural connection:** Respectful of heritage, craft, and
    meaningful occasions.
3.  **Inclusivity:** Fashion for men and women, with room for varied
    tastes and life stages.
4.  **Quality-mindedness:** Present details, materials, and finishing
    clearly; never make unverified quality claims.
5.  **Personal expression:** Help customers find pieces that reflect
    their own identity.
6.  **Trust:** Clear product information, honest imagery, transparent
    pricing, and helpful service.
7.  **Modernity:** A contemporary shopping experience without losing the
    boutique's warmth.

## 3. Audience and Customer Mindset

Design for customers who may be shopping for: - Everyday elevated ethnic
wear - Festive and celebration clothing - Weddings, family gatherings,
and special occasions - Coordinated looks for couples or families -
Gifts and meaningful purchases - Contemporary pieces inspired by
traditional silhouettes

The audience can include women and men across different ages and style
preferences. Do not portray the brand as women-only.

### Customer feelings to support

The experience should help customers feel: - "This feels special, but
still feels like me." - "I can find something appropriate for my
occasion." - "The details and fit are easy to understand." - "I can
trust what I see online." - "There are thoughtful choices for both men
and women."

### Psychological principles for ethical persuasion

Use design and copy to reduce uncertainty and help customers imagine
wearing the product. Do not manipulate or pressure them.

-   **Emotional relevance:** Connect pieces to moments, identity,
    confidence, and personal style.
-   **Choice clarity:** Use meaningful categories, filters, size
    guidance, and concise descriptions.
-   **Visual trust:** Show fabric, texture, colour, fit, and details
    honestly.
-   **Cognitive ease:** Keep navigation predictable and avoid
    overcrowded screens.
-   **Gentle discovery:** Recommend related products based on useful
    styling context.
-   **Social proof:** Use genuine reviews and customer photos only with
    appropriate permission.
-   **Urgency:** Mention scarcity or deadlines only when factually true
    and verified.
-   **Inclusivity:** Use language that welcomes customers instead of
    prescribing how they should look.

Never use fabricated reviews, fake countdowns, false scarcity, body
shaming, or claims that imply a customer needs clothing to be worthy or
attractive.

## 4. Visual Identity

### 4.1 Overall direction

The identity is **premium, editorial, culturally rooted, and
contemporary**. It combines deep blues, restrained metallic gold, warm
ivory, elegant typography, and carefully selected fashion imagery.

The design should feel like a refined boutique editorial---not a
discount marketplace, wedding invitation, jewellery catalogue, or overly
ornate template.

### 4.2 Logo concept

The logo system consists of: - A decorative "BABAJAN" wordmark - A
smaller, widely spaced "SUMANGALI" line - Deep navy/blue and gold
detailing - Optional fine ornamental rules around the secondary name - A
separate emblem/monogram featuring interwoven forms and male/female
profile inspiration for suitable applications

The identity should represent fashion for all genders. Use the shared
monogram or wordmark as the main identifier rather than a women-only
symbol.

### 4.3 Logo usage rules

-   Prefer the supplied approved logo asset over re-creating the logo
    with live text.
-   Preserve the logo's aspect ratio and original letterforms.
-   Maintain generous clear space around all sides; as a baseline, use
    at least the height of the "S" in SUMANGALI.
-   Use the full-colour logo on white, ivory, or very pale backgrounds.
-   Use the approved light/reversed version on dark navy backgrounds.
-   Use the standalone emblem only where a compact mark is needed, such
    as a favicon, avatar, seal, or small label.
-   Use a single-colour navy or white version for embroidery, stamps, or
    constrained print applications when necessary.
-   Ensure the logo remains legible at its rendered size.

**Never:** - Re-type the logo using a similar font and call it the
official logo. - Stretch, squash, rotate, crop, skew, or add effects to
the logo. - Change the navy or gold arbitrarily. - Place the logo over
visually busy imagery without a suitable clear area. - Add "Wear Your
Story" or any other tagline inside the logo unless a separately approved
lockup exists. - Use the logo as a repeating page background. - place
the logo on a background with insufficient contrast.

**Asset note:** The actual approved logo files should be stored in the
project asset directory (for example, `/public/brand/`). This document
does not create or guarantee the presence of those files. If an asset is
missing, ask for it or use a temporary text placeholder; do not
fabricate a replacement logo.

### 5.2 Colour balance

As a starting point for a typical page: - 55--70% white or warm ivory -
20--30% navy and other blues - 5--10% pale blue surfaces - 2--5% gold
accents

These are directional proportions, not rigid mathematical requirements.
Product imagery may naturally change the balance.

### 5.3 Colour behaviour

-   Use Royal Navy for primary text on light backgrounds and
    high-priority actions.
-   Use Classic Blue for links and secondary interactive states.
-   Use Ocean Blue sparingly; it should not make the brand feel playful
    or tech-focused.
-   Use Gold for fine details, not large blocks of body text.
-   Use Soft Blue for gentle section separation, not every card.
-   Use Ivory to create warmth and an editorial atmosphere.
-   Use Charcoal for long-form body text when it improves readability.

### 5.4 Contrast and accessibility

-   Body text must meet WCAG AA contrast guidance: 4.5:1 for normal text
    and 3:1 for large text.
-   Do not use gold text on ivory or white for small text unless the
    measured contrast is sufficient.
-   Never communicate status through colour alone.
-   Include visible focus indicators and clear hover, active, disabled,
    and error states.
-   Verify all actual colour combinations in the implemented interface;
    do not assume every palette pairing is accessible.

## 6. Typography

### 6.1 Typeface roles

**Primary display typeface:** Cormorant Garamond\
Use for editorial headlines, campaign titles, selected collection names,
and refined promotional statements.

**Secondary typeface:** Montserrat\
Use for navigation, body copy, buttons, product names, prices, filters,
form labels, metadata, and utility text.

**Logo typography:** The logo artwork is custom/stylised. Do not replace
it with Cormorant Garamond or any other font.

### 6.2 Font loading

Use properly licensed font files or a trusted font service. Provide
sensible fallbacks.

``` css
:root {
  --font-display: "Cormorant Garamond", Georgia, serif;
  --font-ui: "Montserrat", Arial, sans-serif;
}
```

### 6.3 Type scale

Use responsive sizing and avoid too many sizes.

  Role                  Desktop guidance   Mobile guidance Typeface
  ------------------- ------------------ ----------------- --------------------
  Hero headline                56--76 px         38--48 px Cormorant Garamond
  Page headline                42--56 px         32--40 px Cormorant Garamond
  Section headline             30--38 px         26--32 px Cormorant Garamond
  Card headline                22--28 px         20--24 px Cormorant Garamond
  Product name                 16--20 px         16--18 px Montserrat
  Body copy                    15--17 px         15--16 px Montserrat
  Supporting text              12--14 px         12--14 px Montserrat
  Button/navigation            12--14 px         12--14 px Montserrat

Use fluid typography where appropriate, but do not allow headlines to
overflow or become excessively small.

### 6.4 Typography rules

-   Use display serif type to create emotion and editorial character.
-   Use Montserrat for clarity and consistent interface behaviour.
-   Keep body copy comfortable to read, with line height around
    1.5--1.75.
-   Use uppercase sparingly, especially for navigation labels, eyebrow
    text, and the SUMANGALI wordmark.
-   Use letter spacing for small uppercase labels, not long paragraphs.
-   Avoid excessive italics, all-caps body text, decorative scripts, and
    multiple unrelated typefaces.
-   Use sentence case for most UI labels and customer-facing content.

## 7. Layout and Design Language

### 7.1 Layout principles

-   Editorial composition with generous whitespace.
-   Clear visual hierarchy and restrained decoration.
-   Consistent alignment and predictable page structure.
-   Product photography takes priority over ornament.
-   Use asymmetry only when it improves storytelling or product focus.
-   Use fine rules, quiet borders, and soft neutral surfaces instead of
    heavy shadows.

### 7.2 Spacing system

Use a consistent 4 px base unit:
`4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 80, 96 px`.

Use larger spacing for editorial sections and smaller spacing for dense
product controls. Avoid cramped cards and uneven gaps.

### 7.3 Corners, borders, and shadows

-   Default corners: subtle, around 2--8 px depending on component.
-   Use square or gently rounded product imagery; avoid exaggerated
    pill-shaped cards.
-   Use thin borders in Border Mist.
-   Shadows should be soft and rare.
-   Buttons may be slightly rounded, but should not look bubbly or
    toy-like.
-   Avoid glassmorphism, neon gradients, thick outlines, and excessive
    floating cards.

### 7.4 Decorative motifs

Use sparingly: - Fine ornamental lines - Abstract woven forms -
Simplified arch silhouettes - Flowing fabric-inspired curves - Subtle
geometric motifs inspired by textile patterns - The brand monogram as a
low-contrast repeat pattern

Motifs must support the garments and not compete with product detail.
Avoid culturally specific symbols unless their meaning and use are
understood and approved.

## 8. Photography and Art Direction

### 8.1 Desired photography

-   High-quality, editorial fashion imagery.
-   Natural posture and believable expressions.
-   Accurate fabric colour, texture, embroidery, stitching, and fit.
-   A balanced mix of men's and women's collections.
-   Traditional architecture or refined neutral settings when context
    adds value.
-   A mix of full-length styling images and close-up detail shots.
-   Inclusive representation across age, body type, complexion, and
    style preference where the product range supports it.

### 8.2 Product photography standards

Every product should have, where possible: 1. A clear primary image. 2.
A full-length or full-product view. 3. A back or alternate angle. 4.
Close-up detail of fabric, weave, embroidery, buttons, or finishing. 5.
A styling or lifestyle image when useful.

Use consistent lighting and cropping within a product collection. Keep
backgrounds quiet and do not let props hide the garment.

### 8.3 Image integrity

-   Never materially alter garment colour, pattern, fabric, fit, or
    detailing.
-   Do not use an AI-generated garment image as if it were a photograph
    of the actual item.
-   AI-generated campaign imagery may be used as conceptual art only
    when appropriate and not misleading.
-   Do not imply a model is a real customer without consent.
-   Use properly licensed imagery and approved releases.
-   Provide meaningful alt text for informative images; use empty alt
    text for purely decorative images.

### 8.4 Image treatment

Prefer natural colour grading, warm neutrals, rich but realistic blues,
and balanced contrast. Avoid heavy filters, artificial skin smoothing,
over-saturated gold, and dramatic effects that distort the product.

## 9. Brand Voice and Tone

### 9.1 Voice in one sentence

**Warm, assured, refined, culturally aware, and personal---never pushy
or pretentious.**

### 9.2 Voice pillars

**Elegant:** Use clear, evocative language without overdecorating every
sentence.\
**Personal:** Speak to the wearer's taste and moments, not just the
product.\
**Rooted and contemporary:** Acknowledge tradition while welcoming
modern styling.\
**Confident:** State product facts clearly and avoid exaggerated
promises.\
**Welcoming:** Address men and women naturally; never make one group
feel secondary.\
**Helpful:** Make sizes, fabric, fit, care, delivery, and returns easy
to understand.

### 9.3 Preferred language

Use words such as: - considered - distinctive - timeless - refined -
expressive - crafted (only where supported) - detailed - versatile -
occasion-ready - thoughtfully styled - contemporary -
heritage-inspired - effortless - personal - elegant

### 9.4 Avoid

-   "Cheapest," "crazy sale," "must-buy now," or aggressive bargain
    language as the default voice.
-   Empty superlatives such as "the best in the world" or "100% premium"
    without evidence.
-   Repeated use of "royal," "luxury," "exclusive," or "elite" in every
    sentence.
-   Body-shaming, gender stereotypes, or statements that dictate what
    people should wear.
-   Artificial scarcity, false discounts, and manipulative urgency.
-   Generic AI-sounding phrases such as "elevate your wardrobe to the
    next level" repeated across pages.
-   Claims about fabric origin, handmade production, sustainability,
    durability, or artisan methods unless verified.

### 9.5 Writing rhythm

-   Headlines: short, evocative, and specific.
-   Supporting copy: one or two concise sentences.
-   Product descriptions: factual first, sensory second.
-   Calls to action: direct and low-pressure.
-   Use exclamation marks rarely.
-   Do not use emojis in the main website interface unless a specific
    campaign brief approves them.

## 10. Copy System and Examples

### 10.1 Homepage hero

**Headline:** Wear Your Story\
**Supporting copy:** Discover considered styles for everyday expression,
celebrations, and the moments you want to remember.\
**Primary CTA:** Explore the Collection\
**Secondary CTA:** Find Your Style

Do not imply that every item is handmade, limited edition, or made from
premium materials unless those facts are verified.

### 10.2 Collection entry points

-   Women's Collection --- "Styles for celebrations, everyday elegance,
    and everything in between."
-   Men's Collection --- "Modern silhouettes and considered details for
    your next occasion."
-   Festive Edit --- "Find a look that feels right for the moments worth
    celebrating."
-   Wedding & Occasion --- "Explore thoughtful looks for ceremonies,
    gatherings, and celebrations."
-   New Arrivals --- "Meet the latest additions to the collection."
-   Coordinated Looks --- "Bring your styles together, your own way."
    Only show this category if coordinated products exist.

### 10.3 Product description structure

Use this order: 1. Product name 2. One-line style impression 3. Factual
overview 4. Fabric and material details 5. Fit and sizing 6. Colour and
design details 7. Occasion or styling suggestions 8. Care instructions
9. Delivery/returns information or a clear link

Example template:

> **A considered look for special moments.**\
> \[Product name\] features \[verified design details\] in \[verified
> fabric/material\]. The \[fit/silhouette\] offers \[accurate fit
> description\], while \[specific detail\] gives the piece its
> distinctive character. Style it for \[relevant occasions\] or pair it
> with \[genuine complementary item\].
>
> **Details:** \[material\], \[fit\], \[colour\], \[care\], \[available
> sizes\].

Replace every bracketed placeholder with verified product information
before publishing. Never leave placeholders on a live site.

### 10.4 Buttons and interface microcopy

Prefer: - Shop Women - Shop Men - View Details - Choose a Size - Add to
Bag - Check Availability - Find Your Fit - View Size Guide - Save for
Later - Continue Shopping - Proceed to Checkout - Track Your Order -
Contact Our Team

Avoid vague buttons such as "Click Here," "Submit" when a more
descriptive label is possible, or "I Need This Now."

### 10.5 Empty, error, and confirmation states

-   Empty bag: "Your bag is waiting for something you love."
-   No search results: "We couldn't find a match. Try another term or
    explore the collections."
-   Size unavailable: "This size is currently unavailable."
-   Form error: "Please check this detail and try again."
-   Order confirmation: "Your order is confirmed. We'll share updates as
    it moves forward."
-   Contact success: "Thank you for reaching out. Our team will get back
    to you as soon as possible."

Use these only where they match the actual system state. Never claim an
order or payment succeeded before the backend confirms it.

## 11. Website Experience Guidelines

### 11.1 Navigation

Keep the main navigation simple. Recommended starting structure: -
Home - Women - Men - Collections - New Arrivals - About - Contact

Add Festive, Wedding, or Coordinated categories only if they map to real
inventory. Do not overload the top navigation.

### 11.2 Homepage content order

A suggested hierarchy: 1. Header with logo, primary navigation, search,
account, and bag 2. Hero campaign with one strong headline and clear
action 3. Women's and men's collection entry points 4. New arrivals or a
featured edit 5. Occasion/seasonal story 6. Selected product grid 7.
Brand story or craftsmanship section, only with verified facts 8.
Customer reviews or social proof, only if genuine 9. Newsletter signup
with a clear value proposition 10. Footer with service, policy, and
contact links

Adapt the order to the actual catalogue and business priorities.

### 11.3 Product listing pages

-   Use consistent product cards and image ratios.
-   Show product name, price, and verified colour or availability
    information.
-   Filters should be useful: category, size, colour, price, occasion,
    and availability where supported by data.
-   Keep sorting understandable.
-   Make selected filters easy to see and remove.
-   Provide loading, empty, and error states.
-   Do not fabricate ratings, stock levels, sale prices, or review
    counts.

### 11.4 Product detail pages

-   Make the product images, name, price, size selection, availability,
    and Add to Bag action easy to find.
-   Display size and fit information before the user commits to a
    purchase.
-   Explain fabric, lining, transparency, stretch, care, and included
    items only when those details are known.
-   Show delivery estimates and return terms only from current business
    rules.
-   Provide keyboard-accessible galleries, selectors, accordions, and
    forms.
-   Make mobile purchase actions easy to reach without covering
    important information.

### 11.5 Men's and women's collections

Give both collections equal design care, visual quality, navigation
prominence, and content effort. Avoid pink-only cues for women or
dark-only cues for men. The shared brand palette and editorial style
should unify the experience.

### 11.6 Trust and conversion

-   Display clear contact details and policies.
-   Show the full cost and relevant delivery information before payment.
-   Explain size selection and return eligibility plainly.
-   Use real customer support details and working links.
-   Do not hide important terms in low-contrast text.
-   Ensure cart and checkout remain visually calm and focused.

## 12. UI Components

### Buttons

**Primary:** Royal Navy background, white text, clear hover/focus
state.\
**Secondary:** White or ivory background, navy border and text.\
**Tertiary/link:** Classic Blue text with visible hover and focus
styles.\
Use gold for a small accent, not as the default button fill with white
text unless contrast is verified.

### Product cards

-   Consistent image ratio.
-   Minimal border or shadow.
-   Product name and price easy to scan.
-   Sale labels only when an actual sale exists.
-   Keep colour swatches accessible and label each colour.
-   Hover effects should be subtle and must not be the only way to
    reveal important information.

### Forms

-   Persistent labels, not placeholder-only labels.
-   Clear required-field indication.
-   Inline error text and accessible error associations.
-   Visible focus states.
-   Helpful input examples only where useful.
-   Confirm before destructive actions.

### Navigation and footer

-   Clear hierarchy and predictable destinations.
-   Mobile navigation must be keyboard and screen-reader accessible.
-   Footer should include verified contact details, customer service,
    policies, and social links.
-   Do not show social icons that lead nowhere.

### Motion

-   Use restrained fades, gentle transitions, and small shifts.
-   Keep most transitions around 150--300 ms.
-   Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
-   Do not delay shopping actions with decorative animation.

## 13. Accessibility and Responsive Behaviour

-   Follow WCAG 2.2 AA as the implementation target.
-   Support keyboard-only navigation.
-   Use semantic headings in logical order.
-   Provide descriptive accessible names for controls.
-   Ensure focus is visible and not obscured.
-   Ensure forms announce errors accessibly.
-   Avoid text embedded in images when real text can be used.
-   Do not use colour as the only status signal.
-   Test at narrow mobile widths, tablet widths, and large desktop
    sizes.
-   Avoid horizontal overflow, clipped product details, and tiny tap
    targets.
-   Aim for touch targets of about 44 × 44 CSS pixels where practical.
-   Respect reduced-motion settings and browser zoom.
-   Ensure the website remains useful with images disabled or slow to
    load.

## 14. SEO and Metadata Voice

SEO content should be natural, specific, and truthful.

### Title format

`[Collection or Product] | Babajan Sumangali`

### Meta description pattern

`Explore [verified category/style] from Babajan Sumangali. Discover details, available sizes, and styles for [relevant use].`

### Image alt text

Describe the visible item and relevant details, for example:
`Navy blue embroidered men's kurta shown from the front.`

Do not stuff keywords, invent product attributes, or write alt text such
as "image of" when it adds no value.

## 15. Social, Email, and Campaign Direction

### Visual direction

-   Use editorial compositions, strong product detail, and restrained
    typography.
-   Mix full-look images with close-up fabric and finishing images.
-   Use blue as a consistent anchor while allowing the actual garments
    to remain visually accurate.
-   Keep the logo clear and appropriately sized.
-   Do not place long blocks of text over busy photographs.

### Caption structure

1.  A brief, evocative opening.
2.  One or two lines about the item or collection.
3.  A useful next step, such as exploring the collection.
4.  Relevant details or tags only when useful.

Example: "Made for the moments you make your own. Explore the latest
styles from Babajan Sumangali and find a look that feels like you."

### Email tone

-   Clear subject line.
-   Personal but professional opening.
-   One main message.
-   One primary CTA.
-   Transparent offer conditions, delivery information, and expiry dates
    where relevant.

## 16. Brand Do and Don't

### Do

-   Keep the blue, ivory, and gold system consistent.
-   Give men's and women's fashion equal prominence.
-   Use elegant serif display type with a clean sans-serif interface.
-   Let real products and accurate photography lead.
-   Write with warmth, confidence, and restraint.
-   Make shopping information easy to find.
-   Use gold as a fine premium accent.
-   Use real, verified product facts.
-   Keep layouts spacious and mobile-friendly.
-   Test contrast, keyboard access, and responsive layouts.

### Don't

-   Use random new colours without a clear reason.
-   Add extra taglines to the logo.
-   overuse ornamental flourishes or gold effects.
-   Use generic luxury clichés throughout the site.
-   Use aggressive countdowns, fake stock messages, or fabricated
    testimonials.
-   Make the brand appear exclusively for women or exclusively for men.
-   Alter product imagery in a misleading way.
-   use tiny low-contrast text for important information.
-   hide prices, policies, sizing, or delivery terms.
-   publish unverified claims about craftsmanship, fabric, origin, or
    sustainability.

## 17. Implementation Guidance for AI and Cloud Coding Agents

When implementing or updating the website:

1.  **Inspect the existing project first.** Identify the framework,
    routes, components, asset locations, CSS approach, and current
    design tokens before making changes.
2.  **Preserve working functionality.** Do not replace the project
    architecture or business logic merely to match the visual style.
3.  **Centralize tokens.** Put brand colours, typography, spacing,
    radii, shadows, and motion values in a shared theme or token file.
4.  **Create reusable components.** Use shared Header, Footer, Button,
    ProductCard, ProductGrid, SectionHeading, FormField, and related
    components where appropriate.
5.  **Use real assets.** Check for approved logo files and product
    images. Do not silently substitute generated logo artwork or fake
    products.
6.  **Use real data.** Do not invent prices, inventory, discounts,
    reviews, ratings, sizes, materials, shipping promises, or return
    rules.
7.  **Build responsive-first.** Confirm mobile, tablet, and desktop
    behaviour.
8.  **Maintain accessibility.** Use semantic HTML, keyboard access,
    clear focus states, proper labels, and contrast checks.
9.  **Keep interactions functional.** Search, filters, size selectors,
    cart, account, and checkout controls must perform real supported
    actions. Do not create decorative controls that appear functional
    but do nothing.
10. **Respect backend contracts.** Do not alter API payloads,
    authentication, payment flows, or database schemas without
    understanding the existing contract and testing the change.
11. **Use graceful states.** Include loading, empty, error, and success
    states based on actual application state.
12. **Avoid unnecessary dependencies.** Prefer the project's current
    stack unless there is a clear technical reason to change it.
13. **Validate before completion.** Run available linting, type checks,
    tests, and production build. Fix regressions caused by the change.
14. **Report honestly.** Summarize what changed, list files touched,
    note tests run and their outcomes, and disclose any remaining
    issues.

### Non-negotiable decision hierarchy

When requirements conflict, use this order: 1. Security, privacy, legal
requirements, and data integrity 2. Existing product requirements and
correct business logic 3. Accessibility and responsive usability 4. This
brand guideline 5. Individual visual preferences or decorative
enhancements

Brand styling must never break core functionality, accessibility, or
truthful product representation.

## 20. Final Instruction

Treat this document as the single source of truth for the Babajan
Sumangali brand experience. The intended result is a fashion brand that
feels **refined, welcoming, culturally connected, contemporary, and
trustworthy**.

Every design and content decision should help customers discover
clothing with confidence. The interface should feel premium because it
is clear, consistent, thoughtful, and honest---not because it is crowded
with gold, ornate effects, or exaggerated claims.

When uncertain, choose the simpler, clearer, more inclusive option that
preserves the brand's navy-and-gold identity and keeps the garments at
the centre of attention.
